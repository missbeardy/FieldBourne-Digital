# How a FieldBourne review sounds

This sample is from a real review of a blinds and shutters business. Names and numbers are changed. Use it for tone and sentence length, not as a template to copy.

---

Thanks for your time. I went through the site the way a homeowner would, on a computer and then on a phone. These notes are yours. Use them however you like.

The business under the site is in good shape. You have a clear offer, a showroom in town, a licence number, and a real range of indoor and outdoor work. The website asks people to read a long way before they can book.

The homepage opens on a living-room photo and a script headline. There is no button on that first screen. The phone number is the real next step. It is written out as a heading several times down the page. It does work as a tap-to-call link, which is the right idea. It is still not a way to book.

The free measure and quote page explains the visit in a long write-up. There is no form on that page. The only form sits on the contact page, and it asks for a full street address, suburb, state, postcode and country before anyone can ask for a quote. You already come to the house to measure. Name, phone, suburb and what they want is enough to book the visit.

The product photos look like generated rooms, not jobs done locally. People buying blinds want to see real windows in real houses.

What I would change first:

1. Put one action on every page: **Book a free measure**. Keep the form short: name, phone, suburb, and whether the job is indoor or outdoor. Keep the phone number as a tap-to-call button that stays visible on a phone.
2. Swap the generated room photos for real installs, labelled by suburb. Put two or three reviews under the first screen.
3. Fix the small errors: the misspelt footer heading, the mixed spelling of aluminium, and the old copyright year.

The colours and the logo can stay. The site needs a shorter page, real photos, and a form someone can finish on a phone.

---

## What makes it work

- Opens with thanks and a plain statement of what was done.
- Says something true and good about the business before any problem.
- Every problem is something you can see, described the way a customer would hit it.
- Fixes are actions, with the exact wording or fields where it helps.
- Short sentences. No adjectives doing the work of evidence.
- Ends by saying what can stay. No sales pitch.
